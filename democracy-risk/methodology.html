How this tracker works

Democracy Risk & Institutional Resilience

Each week, this tracks two things about U.S. democracy: how much pressure it's under, and how well courts, states, the press, and other institutions are pushing back. The two are scored separately on purpose — conflict between the executive and a state, for example, isn't automatically bad news if a court steps in and the state wins.

The two scores

Democracy Risk Index (DRI)
0–100, higher = worse

How much pressure, abuse of power, and backsliding is happening.

Institutional Resilience Index (IRI)
0–100, higher = better

Whether checks and balances are actually holding the line.

What DRI looks at

  • Election manipulation (weighted highest) — attempts to distort or delegitimize how elections are run or their results.
  • Agency weaponization — law enforcement, prosecutors, or regulators used against political targets.
  • Judiciary attacks — pressure on judges and courts to rule a certain way.
  • Media retaliation — subpoenas, threats, or legal pressure aimed at journalists and outlets.
  • Corruption and impunity — self-dealing, favoritism, and officials escaping accountability.
  • State-level conflict — coercive standoffs between the federal government and states.
  • Narrative warfare — sustained efforts to undermine shared facts about elections or the law.

What IRI looks at

  • Judicial independence — do courts rule independently, and are their rulings actually obeyed?
  • Election-administration integrity — can election officials do their jobs without interference?
  • State-level resistance — do states successfully defend their lawful authority?
  • Accountability enforcement — does misconduct lead to real consequences?
  • Media and watchdog capacity — can journalists and inspectors general still expose problems?
  • Civil-service independence — do career officials keep their professional protections?
  • Legislative oversight — does Congress actually constrain executive overreach?

The Resilience Gap

Gap = IRI − DRI. A positive gap means resilience is currently outpacing pressure; a negative gap means pressure is winning. It's a quick summary, not a replacement for looking at both scores.

Good to know

  • Scores are careful judgment calls based on evidence, not a scientific instrument — a one-point move only happens when the evidence actually supports it.
  • Resilience is judged by real outcomes (a court ruling actually enforced, a policy actually reversed) — not by officials simply saying the right things.
  • Ordinary political conflict isn't scored as democratic decline unless it actually affects courts, elections, the press, or the rule of law.
  • The method is applied the same way regardless of party in office.
