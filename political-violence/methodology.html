How this tracker works

Political Violence & Social Cohesion

This tracks politically or ideologically motivated violence and threats in the U.S. — and separately, whether the social fabric (trust, leadership, law enforcement response) is holding things together or fraying. It's a companion to the Democracy tracker, but focused on society and violence rather than government institutions; the same event can touch both, but each is scored on its own lens, not double-counted.

The two scores

Political Violence & Extremism Index (PVEI)
0–100, higher = worse

How much politically motivated violence, threats, and extremist activity is happening.

Social Cohesion Index (SCI)
0–100, higher = better

Whether trust, leadership, and institutions are containing that pressure.

What PVEI looks at

  • Politically motivated violence (weighted highest) — attacks, assassination attempts, or plots targeting people over political or ideological beliefs.
  • Threats against officials and judges — credible threats aimed at elected officials, judges, or election workers.
  • Hate crimes — violence or intimidation targeting people for who they are.
  • Extremist mobilization — organized activity by militias or extremist groups, online or in person.
  • Incitement and normalization — rhetoric from prominent figures that encourages or excuses violence.
  • Protest-related violence — violence that breaks out around a demonstration, distinct from the protest itself.
  • Weapons and plot activity — caches, foiled plots, and related law-enforcement actions.

What SCI looks at

  • Law-enforcement and prosecutorial response (weighted highest) — are threats and attacks actually investigated and charged, regardless of the perpetrator's politics?
  • Cross-partisan trust — how much people across the political spectrum trust each other and shared institutions.
  • Leadership rhetoric — do prominent figures condemn violence on their own side, not just the other side's?
  • De-escalation and bridging efforts — organized work to lower the temperature across divides.
  • Civic engagement — participation in normal, peaceful civic and political life.
  • Local news and information ecosystem — whether communities have reliable, non-inflammatory local reporting.
  • Institutional trust in elections and courts — public confidence that these institutions are fair.

The Cohesion Gap

Gap = SCI − PVEI. A positive gap means the social fabric currently has more strength than the violence pressing on it; a negative gap means the pressure is winning.

Good to know

  • The same evidentiary bar applies no matter who the perpetrator or target is politically — an attack isn't scored more or less seriously based on which side it favors.
  • Lawful protest is never scored as violence on its own; only actual violence, credible threats, or organized extremist activity counts.
  • Advocacy groups (on any side) are treated as sources with a point of view, like any other — their data is used, but corroborated where possible.
  • An isolated incident by a disturbed individual is treated differently from an organized, systemic campaign — the tracker distinguishes the two rather than lumping them together.
